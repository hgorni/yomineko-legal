Yomineko Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026. Developer: Nihongator. Contact: gorni.apps@gmail.com.

Short version. The app has no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and what you study stays on your device. Nothing leaves it unless you turn on cloud translation (with your own key or the Yomineko Cloud subscription); then only the area you selected is sent for translation, and the developer keeps none of it on any server. See also the Terms of Use.

Data stored on your device

Words, kanji, review history, notes (including photos you attach), achievements and settings are stored locally. If you use Android's backup, Google may back up this data to your Google account under Google's own terms. API keys you enter are excluded from the backup and are stored encrypted, with a key kept in Android's secure keystore.

To keep the translation of one reading consistent with the previous one, the app also keeps on your device the last 200 passages you read with their translation. Answers the cloud gave (with the cloud mode on) are kept on the device too, so the same question is never sent twice. Settings > Erase everything clears all of this together with your words. The study database, including these passages, is part of the Android backup described above.

Google Drive sync (optional)

If you turn on Settings > Backup > Google Drive sync, you choose a Google account and allow the app to use its own hidden folder in that account's Google Drive (the "app data" folder: the app sees only this folder, not your other files, and the folder does not show in your Drive). The app then keeps there one copy of what is stored on your device as described above (words, kanji, reviews, notes and their photos, the photo gallery, game and practice progress, achievements and settings), and when you open or leave the app joins that copy with what is on this device, so your other devices get what you studied here. The copy is kept in a few files (the study, the answer history of each month that has ended, and the photos), so that less goes over mobile data: the history of a month that has ended and the photos are sent only on Wi-Fi or another network without a data cap. To make a deletion reach them too, the copy also keeps a record of what you deleted (which word, kanji, deck or photo, and when). The copy goes straight from your device to your Google Drive, under Google's terms; it never passes through the developer, who cannot see it. API keys are never included. You can turn sync off at any time and delete the copy from there; you can also remove the app's access in your Google account (Security, Third-party apps), and its hidden data in Google Drive (Settings, Manage apps).

Screen capture, photos and camera

When you tap the floating button the app asks Android for permission to capture your screen and reads the text in the area you select. While the bubble is on the screen that capture session stays open so the next tap is quick, but nothing is recorded or kept: a frame is only taken when you tap, and discarded after it is read. The same happens for photos you take or choose, and for the on-device object recognition that names things in a photo. By default all of this happens on your device (Google ML Kit and an object labeler model bundled with the app) and screen captures are discarded. ML Kit itself sends Google anonymous usage and performance metrics (device model, app version, feature, sizes and error codes, with an identifier per installation); it never sends the pictures or the text it reads. Photos you take with the camera are kept in the app's own gallery, with the text read in them, unless you turn that off in Settings; you can delete them there at any time. When you correct the text the app read, the picture and both versions of the text are kept on your device to measure the reader. If you turn on the optional cloud mode described below, the part of the picture you selected is sent with your own API key to the provider you chose, to read the text in it, and so is a photo when you ask the app to name the things in it. The whole screen is never sent, only the part you select or tap.

Microphone (Shadowing, Pronunciation, Say it)

The app asks for the microphone only where you speak to it: in Shadowing while Repeat along is on, in Say it and compare in a word's entry, in the sheet of a kana in the kana table, under Easy to mix up, when it is your turn, in the Say it game, and when you reply aloud in Conversations. It listens only while you say the line or the word, and stops by itself when you finish. The sound stays in the device's memory to compare it with the guide (its melody, its rhythm and what was said) and, where the screen offers it, to play it back to you; it is never written to a file, never kept after the line is checked or after you leave the screen, and never sent anywhere.

To understand what you said in Shadowing, the Say it game and Conversations, the app uses only the speech recognizer that runs on your phone (Android 13 or newer, with the Japanese pack installed in Android's settings). The app never uses a recognizer that sends audio to a server; without one on the phone, these checks are simply left out.

Translation

Without the optional cloud mode, the app does not translate whole sentences: it explains them word by word with the dictionary built into the app, on your device. When you search the dictionary in your own language, Google ML Kit turns your words into Japanese on your device; the first time, Google downloads a language model to your device. To read what you write with your finger in the dictionary, Google ML Kit downloads a Japanese handwriting model (about 20 MB) by itself in the background the first time the app opens (on Wi-Fi, or on mobile data only if you agree); after that your strokes are read on your device and sent nowhere.

Optional cloud translation

If you turn it on (the app asks you to confirm once) and enter your own API key, the cloud becomes the default for reading and translating: the part of the picture you select, the Japanese text, nearby earlier text used as context, and your style notes are sent to the provider you choose (Anthropic or OpenAI) using your key, and are governed by that provider's terms and privacy policy. The English dictionary meanings of the words you read are also sent, to be translated into your language; those translations are kept on your device so they work offline later. In Write it, what you type in Japanese is sent with the task it answers, to get a correction. If the cloud fails, the app falls back to the word-by-word explanation on your device and says why. This is off by default. The key is stored only on your device.

Yomineko Cloud subscription

With the subscription you do not need a key of your own: the same content described above (the part of the picture you selected, the Japanese text, nearby context, your style notes and the meanings of the words) goes to the developer's server on Google Cloud, which checks with Google Play that the subscription is active and passes the request to the AI provider the developer uses for Yomineko (Anthropic or OpenAI) with the developer's key. The server returns the answer and keeps no content: it does not log the text or the image. It keeps only daily usage counters (number of requests and of tokens), tied to a one-way hash of the subscription's purchase token, so the fair-use limit works; these counters are deleted after 40 days. If a subscription sends many requests the server refuses (malformed requests, too fast, over the limit), the server also keeps, under the same hash, how many were refused each day and the Google Play order id, so the developer can find and cancel a subscription that is being abused; these records hold no text or image either. The server may also ask Google Play Integrity to confirm that the request comes from the real app installed from Google Play; that check is done by Google and sends no content. Under Anthropic's and OpenAI's policies, API requests are deleted within 30 days and are kept longer only for abuse or legal reasons. The subscription is bought, renewed and cancelled through Google Play.

Purchases

Purchases are handled by Google Play. The app only checks whether a purchase exists.

Permissions

Display over other apps (floating button), foreground service and screen capture (reading the screen on request), notifications, camera (taking photos through your camera app), microphone (only while you say a line or a word to the app: Shadowing with Repeat along, Say it and compare, Pronunciation in the kana table, the Say it game and Conversations), internet (model downloads, optional cloud translation, optional Google Drive sync) and access to AnkiDroid (only when you send a deck to it; the cards go from this app to AnkiDroid on the same device).

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.

Changes and contact

Reporting a problem: the flag next to an exercise or a sound, or the "Report a mistake" action opens your own mail app with a message already written (what the item is, or the word and the sound file when you report a sound, the app version, your Android version, phone model and language). The app sends nothing itself; the message leaves only if you press send in your mail app, and then it goes to the developer's address above. When the app crashes it keeps, on the device only, a technical report (the error, the last lines the app wrote to Android's log, your phone model, Android version and memory); it is sent only when you tap "Send report" in Me and choose how to share it.

If this policy changes, the date above changes and the app tells you. Questions: gorni.apps@gmail.com.
